# Progress log

## 2026-11-27: the people on a tape

- Asked for: use strata-models' new face tracking and recognition, with a stage after ingest that waits until every face is named or skipped, so the descriptions can say who does what; and to consider what other tracking the spec needs.
- **Tracks, not keyframe faces.** strata-models' `face-tracks` follows each face through its shot (ByteTrack at 10 fps), so one person in one shot is one thing to name, with a few of its best stills. Asked with the shot starts as cuts, so a track never runs across a cut. The stills are kept in strata-models' face store under the tag `strata-reels:<tape>`, which is opt-in: a search by another app does not see a family's faces.
- **Naming is a gate.** The face store groups the tape's tracks by their mean embeddings (average linkage, never two tracks seen at the same moment) and suggests a person from the faces confirmed before. The person answers a group at a time: this is <suggested>, a new name, someone named before, skip, or not faces. A track whose faces are all under the store's bar (too small or unsure) is never asked about. The gate approves itself once nothing is left; "Skip the rest" approves it by hand. Names live in the face store (identities shared across apps); a skip is this app's override. Every answer is also logged as an override, which is the `cast` stage's input, so a name given after the gate still reaches the scenes.
- **Who does what.** The cast says who is in each scene, seconds on screen and where on the picture (left, middle, right; near or far by face size) per keyframe. The describer's new preset `scene-describe-v3` is told those names per frame, may name only them, and answers `who: [{person, does}]`. Because of that the describe step, and so the chapters, wait for the names: the tape page's finding points to the People tab meanwhile.
- **What else to track** (recommended order, in the plan under R2): diarization and the active speaker first, then sound events, then whole-person tracks for reframing. A general object detector is not needed now.
- Not measured: the grouping's threshold and the suggestion's bar are strata-models' defaults; no real tape has been named yet.

## 2026-11-27: the first automatic reel

- Asked for: the reel stored as revisions, and a simple picker that fits scenes to a target length and keeps the person's locks and bans when it plans again (the gapless preview and the ffmpeg render are a parallel piece of work on the same model).
- **Revisions and proposals** (overview §6). A reel is a handle (its tape, name and the length last asked for); its revisions never change. A proposal is a revision not accepted yet, on the revision that was current when it was made; accepting checks, under the reel's row lock, that the base is still current, so two proposals on one base cannot both be accepted (the second is a 409 to plan again). Each revision records the planner (name, version, seed, parameters), the cache keys of the results it read and the ids of the overrides it obeyed, which with `planner.plan` being pure is enough to make it again.
- **The baseline planner.** A scene's worth is the describer's people (+1) and children (+0.5), its speech (+1 at 20 words, less in proportion) and its picture (+0.5 good, -1.5 poor), each kept as a reason in words. Each clip already taken from a chapter takes 0.75 off that chapter's other scenes, so a short reel visits the occasions instead of the most talkative one. The best go in until the length is filled, the last one shortened to fit, down to 3 s; they play in tape order. A scene loses half a second at each end when it can spare it (the camcorder starting and stopping); a long one is cut to 10 s around the most speech that fits without cutting a phrase, else its middle. Every cut is on a frame (on PAL, half a second is not: it becomes 13 frames at the start and 12 at the end). A seed other than 0 moves each score by up to 0.3 either way, for another version. None of these numbers is measured: they are to be fitted from reels a person has corrected, as the scenes' were.
- **The person's word.** Pin (this scene is in), ban (this scene is out) and lock (this clip stays exactly) are overrides on the reel, read by every plan. A pin or ban names the scene's span, not its number, so it survives the tape being grouped again. When a ban meets a pin or a lock on the same scene the later one holds and the other is listed as a conflict on the revision; locks and pins longer than the target are all kept, with a conflict that says so. Pinning a banned scene (or the reverse) takes the old word back.
- Faces are left out (as agreed): the planner reads the describer's counts of people and children, not the faces step.

## 2026-11-25: correcting the chapters

- Asked for: let the person correct the chapters on the tape page, then any updates the page needs, then run reels as a service. On the real tape the first grouping named a chapter "Christmas nativity play" from a misheard line, put the house and a church hall in one chapter, and split the opening sofa footage in two: all three are corrections a person makes in seconds and a model will keep getting wrong.
- **What the person can say.** At a scene inside a chapter: a chapter starts here. On a chapter: it belongs with the chapter before (a mark on its first scene). On a chapter: its own title. Each mark shows on its scene with Undo mark, which gives the boundary back to the grouping. A chapter can only start at a scene boundary: if the house and the hall are one scene, the scene boundary is wrong first (scene corrections are still to come).
- **Kept as overrides, never edits** (overview §6). A mark is an input of the `chapters` stage (`chapter_marks`), so the tape's job groups again and the stage records say why it ran. The grouping follows every mark (confidence 1 or 0) and keeps what the signals said (`guess`) and the signals themselves beside it. Names are now keyed on the scenes and what the namer is told, not on the grouping, so a regroup asks names only for the chapters that changed. A title is laid over the namer's when the chapters are read, and the namer's stays in sight under it.
- **The check is a gate.** `check_chapters` follows `name_chapters`; the page's "Confirm the chapters" (and Approve on the Steps tab) is strata-python's approve route. It holds until the names it was given change, so a regroup asks again. Nothing waits on it yet: it is the tape's "to check" state (a tape's run is `waiting` until then), and a confirmed tape is a whole tape of labels.
- **Fitting.** `./app fit-chapters` collects every marked boundary, and every boundary of a confirmed tape, with the signals stored in the grouping, and fits `group`'s own parameters by logistic regression, held towards today's by a prior so that a handful of marks moves them a little. It prints the agreement before and after and the new numbers; a release takes them into `PARAMETERS`, as the scenes' were taken from the first real tape. No tape is marked yet, so the weights have not moved.
- Releases: strata-python 0.2.19 (no change reels uses: `wer.find`), strata-react 1.3.15. strata-react's next release makes its default look part of `strata.css` (`UPGRADING.md`, 2026-11-25); reels has its own theme in `app.css` and should then switch its import to `strata.bare.css`, or decide to take the default look.

- A chapter is a run of neighbouring scenes of one occasion: Christmas morning, then dinner. Scenes were already split where the pictures change, so chapters need more than pictures. It is text only: up to 24 of the chapter's scenes, each with its description and up to 400 characters of speech. It answers a title, a summary, guesses with their evidence, tags, and `mixed` when the scenes look like more than one occasion. The page shows `mixed` as a doubt. The grouping stays deterministic code here, the spec's simple baseline first; whether the namer should also judge the boundaries is for when there are marks to measure it on.

## 2026-11-24: the tape's analysis as one pipeline

- Asked for: update to the latest strata-python and strata-react and make full use of what they gained. strata-python 0.2.17 added `workflow` (a `Pipeline` of named, versioned stages over one subject, its records in an append-only table, one queue job per subject, shared routes) and `request_key`; strata-react added `useWorkflowRun` and `WorkflowSteps` to draw it. Jonathan chose to move the job chain onto the pipeline now rather than later.
- Before: eleven kinds of job, each queueing the next (the hash queued the assets, the shots and the speech; the shots the keyframes; they the scenes; the scenes the faces and descriptions), and the describe job had to wait for the transcript by queueing itself again, with a race fixed by not deduping. After: one `tape` pipeline (`jobs.TAPE`), one job per tape that runs each stage not done for the tape's inputs. A stage names what it needs, so describe simply needs the transcript and the race is gone; a failed stage blocks only what needs it, the job fails, and the queue's retry starts at the failed stage.
- The results table stays the cache (spec §6): a stage's record holds the id and key of the result it stood on, so the stage after it runs again only when that is another result. A derived stage's version is its recipe's key, so a changed recipe makes it again.
- One job per tape holds the `disk` resource for the whole run, so two tapes are never read from one drive at once, but a tape's GPU stages no longer overlap its ffmpeg ones. Fine for a home queue; worth looking at when a tape's analysis is long enough that the order matters.
- The tape page gained a Steps tab (`WorkflowSteps`: each step's state in words, a failure in the server's words, Try again) with the job's progress over it while it runs, and the finding names a failed step. The jobs page names each job "Analyse <tape>", its progress the step it is on.

- Faces: boxes and detection scores on every keyframe; no embeddings, so nobody can be recognised before R2 asks who may be. A scene's count is the most faces on one of its keyframes, a floor: a keyframe is one frame of a shot.

## 2026-11-24: shots and scenes (R1 step 2)

- Asked for: start the next step of the plan, turning a tape into scenes.
- Shots come from strata-models' `shot-boundaries` (ffmpeg's scene score after yadif, the role's baseline since strata-models #102). Its answer gives each cut's frame, so the frame is what is stored and every time is frame over the stream's rate, exact. Black stretches are snapped to frames, and a shot that is 90 % black is marked as a gap.
- Grouping: what says two neighbouring shots on a home tape belong to one event is that they look alike (the same room after the camcorder was restarted) and that there is no black between them. Each shot gets one keyframe, its middle frame, made with strata-python's new `derive.frames` (strata-python #69), because a VHS shot is often shorter than the thumbnails' 10 s step. strata-models' `image-embed` (CLIP ViT-B/32) gives each keyframe a vector, and neighbouring shots are compared by cosine. The confidence that a cut starts a scene is a logistic around a cosine of 0.82, 0.04 wide; black at or between the shots raises it to at least 0.9. These numbers are guesses, not measurements.
- Spec S5 asks for a hierarchy of boundaries with a confidence and a reason, not a flat partition. So every cut is kept with its level (`scene` at a confidence of one half or more, else `shot`), its confidence, the similarity and its reasons in words ("pictures alike (0.91)", "black at the cut"). The grouping is a pure function stored as a result (role `reels-scenes`, version 1) keyed on the shots and embeddings it read.
- Checked: the unit suite with strata-models stood in for over real HTTP, and an integration test that cuts real keyframes from a real file (red, dark red, black, blue) and compares their real average colours in place of CLIP. Red and dark red come out as one scene, black as the gap, and blue as another.
- Not done: no run on a real tape yet. The plan's other prerequisites also wait for the GPU machine: `shots-spliced` on the real race footage, and a check of the shot detector on tape faults. The thresholds need a tape whose scenes a person has marked, and step 4's chapters screen makes such corrections. No API for scenes yet; it comes with the chapters screen, its first reader. A shot under a second between two shots of one place (a glitch at a record-pause) ends up as a scene of its own; whether that happens on real tapes is to be seen.
